## Notes — the `RATE LIMITED` verdict above is a false positive

The script printed `RATE LIMITED` this morning. It is wrong, and it has been wrong on every
run since 08-02. The verdict does not survive the evidence:

- **New properties keep getting certificates.** `au-bushfires` (08-03), `scrubjay` (08-03)
  and `loraprep` (08-04) are all `approved`. If the shared 50/week bucket were exhausted,
  they could not be — the limit is per registered domain and they are on `benrichardson.dev`
  like everything else. Burn is ~33.9/50, i.e. ~16/week of headroom.
- **Nine properties held an undisturbed authorization for 42h and never issued.** They were
  cycled 2026-08-02 14:16 and, because the cooldown held through the 08-03 run, were not
  touched again: `noisewell au-inflation au-baby-names wavewell vic-rents facet castwell
  skein tiltwell`. Two full days of in-flight authorization with zero movement is not a
  queue waiting on refill.

**Where the false positive came from.** `pollCerts()` was called *only* on the properties
cycled seconds earlier, and declared `frozen` when their states didn't change across two
30s polls. Freshly-cycled certs always look frozen in a 60s window — issuance takes far
longer. Re-checking the six cycled at 08:12 at 08:37 showed two had already advanced
(`new` → `authorization_created`), while the run had reported them rate-limited.

Fixed in `lab@scripts/ssl/fleet-ssl.mjs`: the intra-run poll is now opportunistic only and
never returns a throttling verdict. Throttling is now judged cross-run — a property cycled
at least a full cooldown ago that still has no certificate is reported as `NOT ISSUING`.

## Leading hypothesis — hostname-scoped, not domain-scoped

Everything ruled out above is domain- or config-scoped, and every domain-scoped explanation
predicts new properties would also fail. They don't. That points at a limit scoped to the
individual hostname — Let's Encrypt's **failed-validation limit** is the obvious candidate:
it is per-hostname, it is triggered by repeated failed authorizations, and each new failed
attempt refreshes the block. The pre-2026-08-02 era, when the factories shipped properties
with domains flapping and authorizations never completing, is exactly the condition that
would have accumulated those failures on precisely this set of hostnames.

**If that is right, cycling is actively harmful** — every cycle creates another failed
validation and extends the block. The current routine cycles six of them per run.
Recommended: stop cycling these nine entirely and leave them untouched for a week to let
the failed-validation counter age out. Not changed unilaterally — this is a policy call.

### Setting the Pages domain does not trigger issuance — cycling does

Worth recording, because it cost ninety minutes. `PUT /pages {cname}` sets the domain but
does **not** open an ACME flow: the property serves fine over HTTP with no
`https_certificate` object at all, indefinitely. The empty→set **cycle** is the trigger.
The factory SKILL.md has always had this as an explicit step (§9 step 4); it was omitted
when scripting the first four renames. `fleet-ssl.mjs` was already correct.

### NEW CEILING — the Cloudflare zone is full (200/200)

`noisewell` could not be moved: `Record quota exceeded` (code 81045). The
`benrichardson.dev` zone is on the **Free plan, capped at 200 DNS records**, and is now at

This is a *second*, nearer ceiling than the Let's Encrypt one documented above, and it
binds first: at ~1 record per property the fleet cannot grow past 200 hostnames, versus
~373 for the certificate limit. At three new properties per day it is already blocking.

The old hostnames were checked as a way to reclaim records and **they are still serving** —
GitHub has not released them, so they do not meet the "GitHub reports it unclaimed" bar and
were not deleted. Freeing that quota needs a decision: upgrade the zone's plan, delete the
14 superseded records deliberately, or move to path-based hosting.
